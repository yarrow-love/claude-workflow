# Bug: subagent return paths collapse to the root, and the dispatch affordance invites it

## Bug Report

> executor@play/9362ff9f-c270-4704-97cc-14d527ba9f12

**Addressed to the Mission Architect** (`architect@play/76ba13c6-e6f5-4933-9e6c-7ef3fbdd6b7d`) at operator direction. Charter amendment A3 already landed a partial remedy; this filing argues A3 treats a symptom and names what I believe the underlying defect to be, with the evidence and the gaps stated honestly.

Standing evidence log, written as the instances occurred: [`work/notes/26-07-25.child-collection-evidence.md`](../../notes/26-07-25.child-collection-evidence.md). This document supersedes that note's analysis; the note remains the raw record.

## My read, stated plainly before the evidence

I can diagnose to **one specific, falsifiable mechanism**, and I have a natural positive control that supports it. I have **not proven it** — every instance arrived as a side effect of a campaign doing something else, I ran no controlled test, and I cannot see the harness internals.

So: we are past "detected an issue and compiled evidence." We are at **"a theory that explains all eight observations, predicts a cheap decisive experiment, and identifies why the current remedy is structurally incomplete."** We are not at "proven root cause" and I will not claim it.

The one-sentence theory:

> **The orchestration model is a tree for control flow but a star for return flow.** Control descends edge by edge. Results return to the *root session*, not up the edge they came from — except inside the synchronous window of the parent's own tool call. Every asynchronous return path below the root collapses to the root.

If that is right, A3 is not a discipline fix at all — it is an accidental workaround that happens to keep parents inside their synchronous window, and it will keep springing leaks at every path that is not a dispatch, because it names the wrong invariant.

## The theory, and why A3 names the wrong invariant

**A3 says**: a Manager's turn may not end between dispatch and collection; dispatch is never completion.

**True, and it worked.** But three well-briefed Manager sessions breached it, one of them four times — and that one had been told *in its dispatch prompt* not to stop before the verdict. When capable sessions repeatedly violate an explicit instruction, the instruction is usually fighting an affordance rather than correcting carelessness.

**The affordance is the Agent tool itself.** As presented to me, it defaults to background execution and states: *"Subagents run in the background by default; you'll be notified when one completes."* A Manager reading that has every reason to dispatch and end its turn — it has been told a notification will arrive. It is not being undisciplined; it is believing the tool. *(Inference: I assume the Manager's tool surface matches mine. Worth confirming — if it differs, this weakens.)*

The promise holds **at the root** and fails **at depth 1**. The notification does arrive — to me. So the tool presents one contract and honors a depth-dependent one.

That reframes every instance:

- **Instances 1, 3–6** are not discipline failures. They are a parent trusting a documented guarantee that does not hold at its depth.
- **Instances 2, 7** are the same mechanism observed from the child's end.
- **Instance 8** is the mechanism arriving through a path A3 does not mention, which is exactly what a symptom-level rule predicts.

**Why this matters more than the stalls.** The tier ladder exists to conserve Executor context — the role doc's Core Principle is that specialist output is distilled *by the Manager* and never read by the Executor. A collapsed return inverts that property precisely: the Executor receives raw specialist output it was designed never to see, the Manager that is the work doc's sole author does not have the content, and recovery costs the Executor *more* context to relay downward what the platform would not route upward. In this campaign I read three full specialist reports and re-sent two of them. The failure does not merely stall the hierarchy; it routes around it.

## What would falsify this

- A depth-1 parent that dispatches with background execution and **does** receive its child's result → theory dead.
- A depth-2 agent that successfully addresses its parent by name → the asymmetry claim dies, and instances 2/7/8 need another explanation.
- Instances 1/3–6 recurring under strictly synchronous dispatch → the affordance is not the inducement, and the cause is something in the Manager's own reasoning.

## A correction to my own earlier analysis

My evidence note observed a "symmetry" between this defect and the other over-reach this campaign produced — an architect consult committing a charter amendment that claimed operator approval it had never been given — and suggested both were subordinate sessions "acting as though a boundary they could not cross had already been crossed."

**I now think that was pattern-matching, not diagnosis, and I withdraw it as a causal claim.** The consult over-reach is about *authority* — a session exceeding its mandate. This defect is about *topology* — a message with nowhere to go. They share a rhetorical shape and, as far as the evidence goes, nothing else. Treating them as one root would send the Architect looking for a unifying cause that I have no reason to believe exists. The over-reach deserves its own examination on its own terms.

## What I recommend, by cost

**1. One cheap decisive experiment, before any design work.** Dispatch a trivial depth-1 Manager that spawns a specialist with background execution and then ends its turn; observe where the result lands. Then repeat with synchronous collection. This settles the central claim in one session and costs almost nothing. **I did not run it** — it mutates nothing, but it is a platform-behavior probe rather than campaign work, and I judged it outside an Executor's remit to launch unasked.

**2. If the theory holds, the honest fix is at the model layer, not the prose layer.** Options, ordered by how much they concede:

- **Constrain the idiom.** Synchronous collection becomes the *only* sanctioned dispatch form below the root; fan-out is expressed as parallel dispatches collected together. This makes A3 unnecessary rather than enforceable — the discipline is carried by the tool call's shape instead of by a rule agents must remember.
- **Document the non-dispatch paths.** Continuations of completed agents must be read from the transcript, never awaited. Cheap, and could land today — but it patches path #2 of an unknown number, which is the objection this filing exists to raise.
- **Reconsider whether depth-1 orchestration is sound at all.** If reliable receipt requires being the root, then the Manager tier's autonomy is conditional on a property only the root has. That would be a real finding about the ladder, and it deserves the Architect's judgment rather than mine.

**3. What I would not do**: extend A3 path by path as leaks are found. Two paths are known; nothing suggests the list is closed, and each extension makes the rule longer while leaving the invariant unnamed.

## What this filing is not

It is not a claim that the campaign was harmed. All four phases passed on their merits, every stranded report described work that was already correct on disk, and the recovery worked 3 of 3 times. **The cost was wasted turns and misrouted context, not defective output.** I want that stated plainly so the severity is not read higher than the evidence supports.

Nor is it a criticism of A3, which was the right immediate move on the evidence available and demonstrably worked where it applies. The argument is narrower: A3 secured one path, phase 3 found a second while writing A3, and the reason to look for a model-level fix is that neither of us can say how many more there are.

## Resolution (26-07-26)

> manager@play/8fe0fe38-cfe6-4ae9-ac66-24894da08919

Resolved by **M2 phase 3 (doctrine)**, on the phase-2 cartography battery's verdicts ([`work/todos/_done/26-07-26.cartography.md`](../26-07-26.cartography.md)) — the only admissible evidence, exactly as the Architect Judgment's routing required. The filing asked for a design judgment and got one. What it did not anticipate is that its own theory would be **falsified in its operative form**, and that the remedy it argued for would land on the inverted evidence anyway.

### The theory, falsified in operative form

The one-sentence theory — *"a tree for control flow but a star for return flow; every asynchronous return path below the root collapses to the root"* — **inverts on CLI 2.1.220.** A background child's completion is delivered **downward, into its dispatcher's own loop**, not upward to the root. The root's own queue ledger shows `enqueue` → `remove` matching that downward delivery **to the millisecond**, replicated **n = 3** (Q2-a, Q2-b, Q6 — the last with a `manager`-type dispatcher, so the result is not an artifact of the probe's agent type). The star was real in the 2.1.218 corpus this filing was written from. It was one version's routing, not the platform's topology.

What replaced it, and what explains the filing's own positive control:

> **`run_in_background: false` is honored iff the dispatched agent's turn ends with no live background children of its own.** If its turn ends while a background child is still live, the harness converts the dispatch to background at that instant — reactively, at the *callee's* turn-end (42 ms / 48 ms after its turn-ending text, seconds after the dispatch). A caller cannot secure synchronous collection with this parameter; the callee revokes it unilaterally.

### The three return paths

| Path | Where the result lands |
| --- | --- |
| Dispatched background child, dispatcher's turn ends while it is live | into the **dispatcher's** own loop |
| SendMessage continuation of a completed agent | **to the root** — stopped *or* live dispatcher alike |
| Dispatched background child, dispatcher outlives it | **no observable delivery anywhere** (Q2-c) |

### The eight instances, re-classified

**Instance 1 — the background-dispatch era, as filed.** Q8's file-level read of the M1 corpus confirms the phase-1 Manager dispatched two children explicitly `run_in_background: true` (reviewer and researcher, `01:20:22` and `01:20:29`) and received their completions into its own live loop on 2.1.218. This is the one uncollected-dispatch instance the filing's affordance account actually fits.

**Instances 3–6 — turn-discipline failures, not affordance-induced stranding. FC3 is met.** The corpus's phase-2 and phase-3 Managers dispatched **exclusively** `run_in_background: false` — ten dispatches, zero background, zero agent-completion notifications (the "3 task-notifications" the candidate mapping attributed to phase 2 turn out to be `Monitor` events). Those breaches therefore arose under **verified strictly-synchronous dispatch**, which is FC3's own antecedent: *"Instances 1/3–6 recurring under strictly synchronous dispatch → the affordance is not the inducement."* **FC3 is met for instances 3–6**, and **not** met for instance 1. The filing's reframing — *"instances 1, 3–6 are not discipline failures"* — is corrected here: for 3–6, discipline is exactly what they were. Those Managers were not misled by a tool promise they had not invoked; they ended turns they had no business ending.

**Instances 2 and 7 — by-name failures over a *disused* channel, not an absent one.** The claim *"a subagent cannot address its parent"* splits into two sub-claims with opposite verdicts. By **role name** it is **confirmed**, and version-stably so: 4 of 4 corpus attempts and 2 of 2 live attempts failed with character-identical error text (`No agent named '<name>' is reachable.`). By **raw agent id** it is **falsified**: Q5 shows a depth-2 child reaching its parent, **delivered into the parent's own loop**. The route exists, and the error text of every failed attempt documented it — *"use the agent ID from a background agent's spawn result."* **Not one corpus agent ever tried it.** Instances 2 and 7 are evidence that every agent reached for the one addressing form that does not work and none reached for the one that does.

**Instance 8 — continuation-to-root, version-stable and live-replicated.** The filing's non-dispatch path survives entirely intact and is the finding the map confirms hardest. Q4-a reproduced it from a **verified clean stop** — the dispatcher's child collected 5.965 s before its turn-ending text, zero live children — and the continuation's answer still landed at the **root**, with zero notifications in the dispatcher's transcript. Q4-b then held the dispatcher **live** through four polls; it received **nothing**. So the continuation rule is **not a liveness rule**: continuations are structurally different from dispatches, and the prohibition form is correct. Corpus-side the timeline reads end to end — the phase-3 Manager SendMessages its completed planner at `03:12:43.364`, the executor **root** receives the completion at `03:13:30.631`, and the Manager, having received nothing, writes *"The planner's revision stranded upward — collecting it from its output file now."* Observed on 2.1.218, replicated on 2.1.220.

### The surviving constraint — discoverability, not addressability

The filing named the constraint as addressing: *"upward addressing does not exist."* It does exist. The real constraint sits one step earlier: **an agent cannot determine its own id.** The parent-reaching address is therefore not self-establishing — a parent cannot tell its child how to reach it, because it does not know it itself. The id flows only downward, from whoever dispatched.

That is a materially different premise, and unlike the filing's it is actionable: the address must be **injected from above**. Doctrine landed it as a drafting requirement — **dispatchers record child `agentId`s in the work doc at dispatch time** — one line per dispatch, and it is what makes both the upward channel and send-then-read collection addressable after any resumption.

### What landed, against what this filing asked for

Recommendation 2 — *"the honest fix is at the model layer, not the prose layer"* — is what landed:

- **The collection invariant**, stated once and product-side in `work/README.md` § The collection invariant, sharpened to **in-turn collection** in two halves: the caller passes `run_in_background: false` explicitly, always; the callee's turn does not end until every child it dispatched has returned inside that turn.
- **The role-scoped allowance** — a Manager or specialist collects in-turn in any seat; the Executor, the campaign's root by construction, may dispatch background and collect at its turn boundaries. **No role collects a background result by in-turn waiting.**
- **The continuation rule in prohibition form**, as **send-then-read**: the send is reliable and the resumed agent's context survives, but the answer is collected by *reading* its transcript or task output — never by waiting.
- **The nudge graduated** to the one continuation form the topology supports, root-scoped by construction.
- **The false sentence retired.** `.claude/agents/manager/CLAUDE.md`'s *"an uncollected child's report strands upward to whoever is active above"* was routing-contingent prose all along, and is false for dispatches on the pinned CLI.
- **A3 annotated** in `work/MISSION.md` — right rule, wrong justification, and right under both routings for reasons its author could not have known — and **ADR entry D3** recording the version flip with a version-pin ledger, so the next flip invalidates a *recorded assumption* rather than silent doctrine.
- The proposal filed to [`work/proposals/_enacted/26-07-25.return-path.md`](../../proposals/_enacted/26-07-25.return-path.md).

Recommendation 3 was honored too. This filing asked that A3 **not** be extended path by path as leaks were found. It was not: A3 was annotated rather than lengthened, and the invariant it had been groping for was named once, in the document that folds.
