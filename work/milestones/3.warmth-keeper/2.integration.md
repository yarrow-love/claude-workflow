### The registration convention — what it must say

Written project-generically, in the grammar's existing vocabulary:

- A **registered** handle is one the keeper holds warm. Registration is keyed on the same `role@machine/session-id` handle the grammar already defines — **not a new addressing scheme** (D3, exactly as the channel was demoted).
- **Register at campaign open, release at campaign close.** `register` is idempotent start-or-attach, so a campaign never needs a separate "start the keeper" step; releasing the last handle lets the daemon self-terminate, so a closed campaign never orphans one.
- **Registration is machine-local**, like the consult it accelerates: the `machine` segment must match this host, and a cross-machine handle is not registrable (charter § Non-goals — cross-machine routing).
- **Warmth is an optimization, never a precondition.** An unregistered handle consults exactly as before, at cold price. Nothing in the consult path may come to *depend* on the keeper running — the fork ladder is unchanged, and the keeper's total failure cost stays bounded at one cold reload.
- **What to register**: the standing consult target for the campaign — in practice the last `architect@…` entry the Manager's Planner Callback policy already resolves as the blessing handle. Registering handles nobody will consult is pure cost at ~$0.23 per beat on a flagship prefix.

### The ADR hazard entry

Title it for the failure, not the feature. The substance: **a registration that is never released beats indefinitely** at roughly $5.60/day on a flagship prefix — an efficiency mechanism inverted into a cost source. The 18-hour dormancy bound is the backstop, not the fix; the fix is releasing at campaign close. Name the live precondition: campaigns die (M2 recorded five consecutive agent deaths), and a dead campaign runs no close-out.

Keep it **version-free**, per the M2 gate ruling that settled the same question for the DENY entry — point at the defaults in the script's comments rather than duplicating numbers that would drift.
