# Consult economy — batching, a digest-first ladder, and a persistent intercom

> Filed 26-07-28 at operator direction, from the evidence in `work/notes/26-07-28.consult-token-cost-evidence.md`. The problem: the v4-era callback intercom (`claude -p --resume <uuid> --fork-session`, via `.claude/bin/consult`) rehydrates the superior's **entire live context into every fork** — a per-consult cost proportional to the superior transcript's size, paid again on every question, at cache-write prices whenever consults are spaced beyond the prompt-cache TTL. Observability improved; tokens and latency regressed by the same mechanism. Nothing here is a work order.

## The tension being optimized

Two goods pull against each other:

- **Observability** — the operator can watch subagent work live in the Claude CLI/Desktop surfaces, and callbacks answer from the author-session's own recorded intent rather than from documents about it.
- **Token economy** — the intercom's cost should scale with the *question*, not with the *superior's age*.

The resume-fork design bought the first by abandoning the second: cost is now proportional to superior-transcript size, which grows monotonically over a mission. The proposal is three remedies in escalating order of ambition. The first two are protocol edits adoptable immediately; the third is the designed successor. They compose — adopting 1+2 does not prejudice 3, and 3 does not obsolete 1+2 (forks remain the right tool for consulting *archived* sessions, which no live channel can serve).

## Remedy 1 — batch questions per consult (protocol edit)

N questions asked as N consults cost N full prefix loads; the same N questions in one consult cost one. The consult contract already allows a compound question — nothing in `.claude/bin/consult` or the callback grammar assumes one question per fork.

**The edit:** consulting roles (Manager, Executor, Blessing) accumulate their questions for a given handle and consult **once per natural boundary** — phase close, verdict formation, blessing — rather than per-question as questions arise. The consult prompt becomes a numbered list; the fork answers in kind.

- Where a question genuinely blocks mid-phase work, it is still asked alone — batching is the default posture, not a gate.
- Secondary effect: batched consults cluster in time, so when a second consult *is* needed shortly after, the prefix is a cache hit (~0.1× input price) instead of a fresh cache write (1.25–2×).

Doc touchpoints: the consult conventions under `work/README.md` § The callback grammar, plus the role cards that instruct consulting (manager, executor).

## Remedy 2 — invert the ladder: digest first, resume as escalation (protocol edit)

The current consult ladder treats documents as the *degrade* path — what you fall back to when the transcript is lost. Invert it: **documents are the first rung; a transcript resume is the escalation.**

Most consults are interpretation questions — "what did you intend by X," "why was Y rejected" — that the mission's own decision record (charter, ADRs, proposal files, campaign logs) answers at a fraction of the cost: a fresh cheap-model dispatch over the relevant documents pays the flat baseline (~31k tokens measured) instead of the superior's whole context. The full-fidelity resume stays available for the minority of questions where the record is genuinely silent and only the session's own context can answer.

**The edit:** a consult first dispatches fresh against the decision record; the fork-resume fires only when the asker (or the fresh dispatch itself) determines the record cannot answer. Two design notes:

- **This strengthens an existing invariant rather than bending one.** The grammar already states a callback is "provenance plus a consult handle, never authority," and that consults are interpretation-only. A question a good decision record cannot answer is disproportionately likely to be a *new decision* in disguise — which escalates to the Operator anyway, not to a fork.
- **It creates a virtuous pressure:** sessions whose intent is expensive to consult are sessions that under-documented their decisions. The cheap rung failing is a signal to improve the record, not just to pay for the resume.

Doc touchpoints: `.claude/bin/consult` (ladder order), `work/README.md` § The callback grammar (Durability bullet).

## Remedy 3 — the persistent intercom (designed successor)

The open-connection ideal the operator named exists in the CLI as **streaming input mode**:

```
claude -p --input-format stream-json --output-format stream-json
```

The session runs as a live process. Stdin stays open; successive user messages arrive as JSON events; each turn appends only the new exchange; back-to-back turns on one conversation keep the prompt cache continuously warm. Cost per exchange scales with the exchange. Observability is equal or better than resume-forks — stream-json emits every event live, and the Executor doc already consumes these events (session-id-at-launch, stall detection).

**Shape:** superior sessions that expect callback traffic (flagship architect during a campaign; Executor during `/execute`) run as held-open streaming processes for the campaign's duration. Inferiors' questions route to the live channel instead of spawning forks. The channel *is* the session — no reload, ever, within its lifetime.

**What it must not lose from the current design:**

- **The read-only consult boundary.** A live superior answering questions is fine; a live superior being *driven* by inferiors is an authority inversion. The channel needs the same interpretation-not-decision contract the grammar states for forks — enforced by protocol at minimum, by a capability boundary if the surface allows.
- **Fork isolation for parallel consults.** `--fork-session` lets parallel consults never touch the original transcript. A single live channel serializes askers; either accept that (consult volume is low after Remedy 1) or keep forks for the parallel case.
- **Archived handles.** A live channel serves live sessions only. Consults against closed missions' archived transcripts remain resume-forks by necessity — the ladder (Remedies 1–2 included) stays for them.

**The new cost — lifecycle machinery, currently gotten for free:** process ownership (spawn, heartbeat, stall-kill, restart after reboot), a routing convention from inferior sessions to the live channel's stdin, and crash semantics (a died channel degrades to the resume-fork ladder — which is why 1+2 stay load-bearing). This is real design work and the reason this remedy is the successor, not the immediate edit. It also naturally reopens the dispatch-model question in `work/notes/26-07-25.child-collection-evidence.md` — a persistent superior is precisely a collection point that cannot be abandoned by a turn ending.

## Adoption order and the measurable test

Adopt 1 and 2 now; hold 3 as the successor design pending evidence that consult volume after 1+2 still justifies a live channel.

The test is cheap and should be recorded in the evidence note: **per-campaign consult count × mean consult cost, before and after 1+2.** If the residual spend is dominated by a handful of unavoidable full-context resumes to a live flagship session, Remedy 3 pays for its machinery; if 1+2 collapse the spend, it does not, and this proposal's third section moves to `_deferred/` on its own.
