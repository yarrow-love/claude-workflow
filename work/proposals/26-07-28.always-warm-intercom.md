# The always-warm intercom — a persistent stream-json channel as the consult backbone

> Filed 26-07-28 at operator direction, superseding `_rejected/26-07-28.persistent-intercom-and-consult-economy.md`. This revision is the basis for the v4.token-efficiency mission charter (`work/MISSION.md`). Nothing here is a work order.

## The problem, restated with the corrected mechanism

The v4-era callback intercom (`claude -p --resume <uuid> --fork-session`, via `.claude/bin/consult`) makes every consult cost proportional to the superior session's age. The evidence note measured the flagship architect transcript at ~60 MB raw — plausibly hundreds of thousands of tokens rehydrated per consult — precisely on the handle where consult traffic is heaviest.

The predecessor proposal diagnosed the *symptom* correctly but the *mechanism* imprecisely. The corrected mechanism:

- **The Messages API is stateless.** Every turn — resume-fork or held-open process alike — transmits the full conversation as input tokens. There is no server-side session that a live connection keeps resident, so no design "avoids the reload." What a design controls is the reload's **price and latency**.
- **The prompt cache is the price mechanism.** Input tokens served from a warm cache bill at ~0.1× with fast prefill; a cold prefix is a cache *write* at 1.25× (5-minute TTL) or 2× (1-hour TTL) with full prefill latency. Claude Code sessions use the 1-hour TTL.
- **The measured regression is coldness, not forking.** The typical intercom rhythm — one consult per phase boundary, spaced beyond the TTL — makes each consult a worst-case cold write of the whole flagship prefix, plus the prefill wait the operator observed as latency. Both reported regressions (tokens and wall-clock) are the same cold-cache event seen from two sides.
- **Corollary (spike-verify):** the cache is keyed on the byte-exact prefix, and a `--fork-session` resume reproduces the superior's prefix byte-for-byte. A fork consult issued *while the superior is warm* should itself hit the cache at ~0.1×. If confirmed, the fork mechanism was never intrinsically expensive — cold spacing was.

The design premise therefore amends from the predecessor's "no reload, ever" to: **always-warm reload at ~0.1×.**

## The resolution: a persistent stream-json intercom

Superior sessions that expect callback traffic (flagship architect during a campaign; Executor during `/execute`) run as held-open streaming processes for the campaign's duration:

```
claude -p --input-format stream-json --output-format stream-json
```

Stdin stays open; inferiors' questions arrive as JSON events on the live channel instead of spawning forks; each exchange appends only the new turn client-side and lands on a warm cache server-side. Cost per exchange scales with the cache-read price of the prefix plus the exchange itself, and prefill latency disappears in the warm case.

**Warmth is a maintained property, not a free one.** Because the win is cache economics, the channel's lifecycle machinery must include a **TTL/heartbeat policy**: regular traffic refreshes the TTL on its own; during quiet stretches the channel keeps itself warm with a trivial heartbeat turn (or a `max_tokens: 0` pre-warm request) at ~0.1× of the prefix — versus 1.25–2× for the cold write every sporadic consult pays today. The break-even cadence is a spike measurement, not an assumption.

### Observability is preserved — and improved

The predecessor framed observability and token economy as a trade-off. Under this design it is not one:

1. **The transcript file is the observability substrate, and it remains.** A held-open `-p` session is still a session: it has a session ID and writes the same `.jsonl` transcript to the local project store that the CLI/Desktop surfaces and the Executor's stall rules already read, appending incrementally as turns happen. (Spike-verify: live append and mid-flight visibility in the Desktop surface.)
2. **stream-json output is strictly more observable than resume-forks.** Every event — tool calls, token counts, subagent activity — emits on stdout as it happens and can be teed anywhere. A fork yields a transcript after the fact; the channel yields a wire you can watch.
3. **Archived handles are unaffected.** When the channel's process ends, its transcript persists and remains resumable; consults against closed missions stay resume-forks by necessity, exactly as today.

### What the channel must not lose

- **The read-only consult boundary.** A live superior answering questions is fine; a live superior *driven* by inferiors is an authority inversion. The channel carries the same interpretation-not-decision contract the grammar states for forks — enforced by protocol at minimum, by a capability boundary if the surface allows.
- **Fork isolation for parallel consults.** A single live channel serializes askers. Either accept that (consult volume is low) or route the parallel case to warm forks — which the corollary above suggests are cheap whenever the channel is doing its job of keeping the prefix warm.
- **The callback grammar.** The handle stays `role@machine/session-id`; the channel is a *transport* for consults against a live handle, not a new addressing scheme. Fork-resume remains the degrade path at every failure: a died channel falls back to the existing ladder.

### Lifecycle machinery — the mission's core deliverable

The fork-per-question design got lifecycle for free by having no state between calls. The channel pays for its economics with real machinery, which is the substance of the mission:

- **Process ownership** — spawn at campaign open, session-id capture from the init event, stall-kill, restart after reboot, teardown at campaign close.
- **Warmth policy** — heartbeat cadence derived from the spike's measured TTL behavior and break-even.
- **Routing convention** — how an inferior session's consult reaches the channel's stdin, and how the answer routes back.
- **Crash semantics** — a died channel degrades to the resume-fork ladder without losing a question.

A persistent superior is also precisely **a collection point that cannot be abandoned by a turn ending** — the dispatch-model defect recorded in `work/notes/26-07-25.child-collection-evidence.md`. The mission should treat that as an expected secondary benefit and record whether it holds.

## Dispositions of the predecessor's remedies

| Predecessor remedy | Disposition |
| --- | --- |
| Remedy 3 — persistent intercom | **Adopted as the crux**, with the mechanism corrected as above. |
| Remedy 1 — batch questions per consult | **Deferred, post-crux optimization.** Larger batched exchanges are more token-efficient and batching concentrates traffic inside the TTL window, so it compounds with the channel — but it is not the root fix. Evaluate after the intercom lands. |
| Remedy 2 — digest-first ladder | **Rejected (operator veto).** Answering consults from a document digest sacrifices answer quality; the purpose of the callback chain of command is that the superior with the highest-level reasoning answers from its own maintained context. |

## The opening spike

The mission begins with a spike test confirming the theorized resolution — a single scripted session on a machine holding a real large transcript:

1. **Hold-open mechanics.** Spawn the streaming process, send two turns, confirm: session-id capture from the init event, live transcript append, mid-flight visibility in the CLI/Desktop surfaces, and clean resume of the same session after process death.
2. **Cost matrix.** Using per-turn `usage` fields (`cache_read_input_tokens` / `cache_creation_input_tokens`), measure the same consult question four ways: (a) cold resume-fork; (b) warm resume-fork, within TTL of superior activity; (c) persistent-channel turn within TTL; (d) persistent-channel turn after TTL expiry. Also measure one heartbeat turn. This yields the heartbeat break-even and confirms or refutes the warm-fork corollary.
3. **Read-only boundary check.** Confirm the channel can answer consults without acquiring the ability to act (permission mode / prompt contract), preserving the consult-is-interpretation invariant.

Measurements append to `work/notes/26-07-28.consult-token-cost-evidence.md`, the standing raw record.

## The measurable test

Per-campaign, from the channel's own emitted `usage` events: **consult count × mean consult cost, and the warm share of consults.** Success is the mean consult cost collapsing from cold-write-of-the-flagship-prefix toward cache-read rates, with the warm share near total; the residual cold events should be enumerable (channel restarts, first-touch after boot) rather than the steady state.
