# Empirical evidence: the child-collection boundary may be a foundational defect

> Filed 26-07-25 by `executor@play/9362ff9f-c270-4704-97cc-14d527ba9f12` at operator direction, during the `/execute` campaign for milestone v2.M1 (janitor). Pre-proposal: observations from one campaign, recorded while fresh. Related: `work/MISSION.md` § Amendments A3 (the phase-3 rule that addresses the symptom), and v1's carried design question "verdict-absence collection".

## Why this is filed rather than left in the campaign log

Charter amendment A3 lands a **rule** for this symptom: a Manager's turn may not end between dispatch and collection. That is prevention at the dispatch site, and it is the right immediate fix. This note records something the rule does not settle — **the observed frequency suggests the workflow's dispatch model may rest on an assumption the platform does not honor**, which is a question above any one mission's scope.

## What was directly observed, this campaign

Two phases, both of which reached PASS on their merits. The build work was never wrong; the *reporting boundary* was.

**Manager turns ending with no `## Acceptance Verdict`, work uncommitted — 5 occurrences:**

| Phase | Turn-end text | State at that moment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | "Waiting on the two nit fixes." | 4 files modified, uncommitted; review already at 0 blocking |
| 2 | "Monitor armed. Awaiting the implementer's resolution of findings 1 and 2." | 2 files untracked, 2 modified |
| 2 | "Monitoring; the resolution pass is still in flight." | unchanged |
| 2 | "Still in flight." | unchanged |
| 2 | "Awaiting the implementer's completion." | unchanged — the implementer had **already finished** |

**Child reports stranding upward past their dispatcher — 2 occurrences**, one per phase. Both times a depth-2 implementer completed its work, could not address its Manager (`SendMessage` has no route from a subagent to its parent by name), and its full report — findings resolved, verification re-run — landed in the **Executor's** context instead. Both implementers said so explicitly and unprompted; the phase-2 one opened with *"`SendMessage` couldn't reach a teammate named `manager` — I'm a dispatched subagent, so my reply channel is this final message."*

**Cost.** Zero rework — every stranded report described work that was already correct on disk. The cost was entirely in wasted turns and in Executor context spent relaying downward what the platform would not route upward. The phase-2 Manager's own post-mortem: *"the landed edits were already correct when I re-read the file — the cost was three wasted turns of monitoring, not rework."*

## The shape of it

The Manager doc's dispatch model assumes a request/response boundary: dispatch a specialist, receive its report, distill it. What the platform actually provides is **collection** — a parent receives a child's result only by remaining in the loop to collect it. A parent that dispatches and then *ends its turn* has not parked a pending request; it has abandoned the collection point. The child still completes, and its report surfaces to whoever is active above.

Two consequences the current docs do not state:

1. **"Awaiting X" is not a state a subagent can occupy.** A Manager that writes it has already lost the thing it is waiting for. The wait is not pending — it is over, and the result went elsewhere.
2. **The Executor becomes an unwilling relay.** Twice this campaign the recovery was: Executor reads the stranded report, re-sends its content *downward* via `SendMessage` so the Manager can act on work it already owned. That works, and it is what the nudge is for — but it means Executor context is being spent on message routing, which is precisely the context the role doc says to conserve for acceptance reasoning.

## Why this may be foundational rather than incidental

A3 frames this as a discipline problem — Managers must not end turns mid-dispatch — and states the rule once with references from both work-order workflows. That is correct and sufficient for the symptom.

The open question is whether **prose discipline is the right instrument for a platform-topology constraint.** Five occurrences in two phases, from three different Manager sessions, each of which had read a work order that explicitly told it to run to a verdict and not stop — the phase-2 order said *"Do not stop before the verdict"* in the dispatch prompt itself, and it stalled four times anyway. A rule that well-briefed agents breach at that rate is worth asking whether the surrounding design is fighting the grain:

- Does the Manager tier need a **dispatch idiom that cannot be abandoned** — e.g. collect-in-the-same-turn as the only sanctioned form, with fan-out expressed as parallel dispatches collected together rather than sequential dispatch-then-wait?
- Should the **Executor's nudge** be documented as a standing operational recovery rather than an exception — it has now worked 100% of the time it was tried, in 3 of 3 attempts?
- Is there a **detection point** earlier than the Executor's read of a stalled ledger? A Manager cannot see that its own child stranded; only the layer above can.

## A second episode in the same campaign — and my error about it

**Corrected 26-07-25, after the mission architect's judgment (`work/proposals/26-07-25.return-path.md`).**

I originally recorded here that an architect consult "drafted and **committed** charter amendment A3 to `work/MISSION.md`, with an authorization line reading 'operator-approved' before the operator had seen it," and called the authorization fabricated.

**That was wrong on the central fact.** The mission architect attests that the operator approved A3 explicitly, in the architect dialog, before the commit. No consult was involved — consult forks are read-only by construction and cannot commit at all, which I should have reasoned from. My inference rested on an unstated and false premise: that because no operator input had reached *my* session since the Reconnaissance gate, none had occurred anywhere. The operator's dialog with the mission architect was invisible to me, and I treated my own view as the whole record.

What survives is smaller and genuinely useful: **the amendment asserted an approval without naming the approving session**, so a mid-campaign observer had no way to verify it. That is a declared-vs-actual gap in the charter grammar — this mission's own failure class, in this mission's own instrument. The remedy is that charter amendments now carry the approving session's callback handle.

I am leaving the error in the record rather than editing it away. A note whose purpose is honest evidence about how this system fails should show its own author reaching a confident wrong conclusion from a partial view — that is the same failure shape as an uncollected report landing at the wrong session, and both are about a session mistaking its local view for the global one.

**And I withdraw the analytical claim I built on it.** I had suggested this and the return-path defect shared a root — subordinate sessions "acting as though a boundary they could not cross had already been crossed." With the facts corrected there is no over-reach to pair with, and the pairing was pattern-matching rather than diagnosis in the first place. The return-path defect stands on its own evidence.

## Status

Open observation. Graduates into a proposal if a later mission wants to revisit the dispatch model; evaporates if A3's rule proves sufficient in practice. The measurable test is cheap: **after phase 3 lands the child-collection rule, does phase 4's Manager still strand?** Phase 4 runs three Manager sessions with heavy specialist fan-out, which makes it a genuine trial rather than a formality — and that result should be recorded here before this note is triaged at mission close.
